Early-Tenure Retention Trigger

A subscription retention feature, designed from real churn data

Role: Product Manager (portfolio project)

The Problem

A subscription business with a month-to-month contract option was losing customers at a company-wide rate of 26.5%. But averages hide the real story: when broken down by segment, one group stood out — month-to-month customers, within their first 6 months, who had not adopted a support add-on — churned at nearly 2.5x the overall rate.

That segment was 966 customers, representing $63.3K in monthly recurring revenue sitting in the highest-risk window of the customer lifecycle.

The Insight

Customers with a TechSupport add-on churned at 15.2%, versus 41.6% without one — a 63.6% relative gap. Whether that's causal or just correlational wasn't something the data alone could answer, so the feature was designed to test that question, not assume the answer.

Without TechSupport 41.6% Churn
With TechSupport 15.2% Churn

The Feature: Early-Tenure Retention Trigger

1
Trigger
Month-to-month contract + 30-45 days tenure + no TechSupport/OnlineSecurity add-on
2
Action
Customer Success places a proactive check-in call and offers a free 30-day trial of the support add-on
3
Suppression
Customers already contacted recently, or with an open support ticket, are excluded
4
Validation
Rolled out as a controlled pilot (50% treatment / 50% control), not a blanket launch

Impact Model (Pilot-Scale)

Metric Modeled Value
Target segment 966 customers
Assumed adoption rate 40%
Customers retained (modeled) ~161

Intervention cost ~$3.1K
Modeled ROI ~40x

Note: Two explicit assumptions drive this model — adoption rate and cost per outreach — both flagged for replacement with real pilot data before any full rollout decision.

"The easy version of this project stops at 'customers without support add-ons churn more, so cross-sell support add-ons.' That's a data observation, not a product decision. The harder, more honest version treats the correlation as a hypothesis, designs a pilot with a real control group to test it, and builds in guardrails before scaling."
